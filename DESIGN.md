# Creator Intelligence — Design v1

Oct 31, 2026 · Huzaifa Javed

## What it is

Creator Intelligence digs up raw material from marketing, behavioural psychology, and founders and company building, from any time, last week or decades ago, and keeps every find in a library that grows and learns your taste. It runs on the World Intelligence chassis, but where World Intelligence tracks what's happening now, this judges a find by how rich and novel it is, not by when it happened.

**Two purposes, both real:**

1. Acclimate you to the three fields. A find is worth keeping even if it never becomes a video.
2. Supply raw material for content: big topics with fresh angles.

**It serves your formula, it doesn't run it:** proven format + big topic + ICP fit + fresh angle + instantly understandable, produced fast. The system supplies the find and the principle. Choosing and bridging stay yours.

**The core idea, from the case study:** the concept is the big topic and the find is the fresh angle. Concepts like friction or identity can be well known; the find that anchors them has to be new.

**Rules:**

- **Volume over depth.** Content is finding and extracting. Nuance doesn't survive short form, and the system writes no hedged framing.
- **Accuracy is not a priority** unless something is blatantly wrong. A source link on every find is the only check.
- **Except quotes.** A quote from a real person must be real, with a real clip.
- **Familiarity matters.** A find can't be so obscure or bizarre it makes no sense.
- **Popular companies are fine;** generic stories about them ("how Apple scaled") are not.
- **All three pools count equally.** It is not a psychology-study finder.
- **It's a brain.** It improves at finding over time, by design. It stays out of the creative step unless asked.
- **Out of scope:** content strategy, including how far a format can be adapted.

## At a glance

```text
brain (taste, strategies, shapes, leads, seen)
   -> daily run (competing strategies dig, writes ~30 cards)
   -> library (cards + concept pages, stored in second-brain)
   -> you (read the daily email, reply on its GitHub issue, search with /creator-intelligence)
   -> reply run (handles "deeper" and "bridge", sends the new emails, logs signals)
   -> weekly run (asks what you used, rescores strategies, trials new shapes)
   -> brain
```

Pull mode reads the same library whenever you ask, so every card the daily run writes stays useful long after its email.

## How it hunts

It searches by shape, not by topic. "Uber growth strategy" returns the generic story; "why does an everyday product do this odd specific thing" returns the chime. Shapes are ways of looking that work across all three pools, which is how it thinks horizontally.

### The three pools

| Pool | Where the material lives | What a lead looks like |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Marketing | Campaign case studies, launch teardowns, ad archives, positioning stories | A campaign, a brand, a launch |
| Behavioural psychology | Papers, experiments, books | A researcher, an experiment, an effect |
| Founders and company building | Company histories, founder interviews, podcast archives, postmortems, biographies | A company, a founder, a pivot, a failure |

The pools are information sources, not content categories. A find doesn't need to touch more than one.

### The shapes

| # | Shape | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Familiar thing, hidden reason: a product, moment or behaviour everyone recognises | The Uber chime |
| 2 | Simple experiment, shocking number | The tapping study, 2.5% |
| 3 | A decision that looked wrong and worked | |
| 4 | Tiny change, outsized result | |
| 5 | Famous outcome, unknown origin | |
| 6 | Day-walk: ordinary objects and moments of a day, each asked "why is it like this?" | |
| 7 | Far-field: an unrelated field whose principle carries over | The surgeon who learned stitching from lace-making |
| 8 | Credible person, unexpected claim | The Olympian crediting neuroplasticity |
| 9 | Unfamiliar concept as the hook: a named idea most people have never heard | Pronoia; identity priming |

- **Unshaped finds still get in,** tagged "unshaped." Clusters of them are how new shapes are discovered.
- **Each run is balanced** across shapes and pools as a hard rule: every pool gets at least a quarter of the cards and no shape gets more than a quarter. The weekly email reports the actual spread, so any drift shows.
- **Quotes (shape 8)** carry the credential or the fame, whichever the source has, in a form ready to say out loud. The source must be a real clip, video or podcast by default, with the timestamp. No clip, no card.
- **Old or new, in every shape.** Age is never a filter. Feeds (new podcast episodes, newsletters, interviews) supply recent material alongside the digging into older material, and both compete on richness and novelty.

### Leads

The system generates its own leads, mostly names: companies, people, campaigns, experiments, concepts. Every find throws off new names, so the queue feeds itself. You can drop a lead in, but nothing depends on it.

### Competing strategies

Several search strategies run side by side, each a named way of looking (a shape aimed at a pool, a dig site, a far field). Weak ones get cut and strong ones spawn variations. The unusual dig sites enter as contestants and get tested instead of debated: court filings, patents, old shareholder letters, trade magazines, ex-employee forum threads, archived websites, dead companies, and practices from other countries.

### The concept library and the two search directions

Every named concept (friction, curse of knowledge, pronoia) is a page that collects every find that anchors it. The system searches both ways:

- **Find → concept:** it finds something and names the idea underneath.
- **Concept → finds:** it takes a proven concept and hunts for fresh, concrete evidence of it. This is the formula done as research: a big topic, freshly anchored.

"Never twice" applies to finds, not concepts. Concepts are meant to repeat; that is what a big topic is.

### Cost

Cost is not a constraint. The featured finds get full research every day, and anything you ask to deepen gets the same.

## The card

A card is one find in a fixed short format. Every card is the same size, and it describes the find without steering toward a take.

```text
The find:      what happened or what it is
The surprise:  why it's not what you'd expect
The idea:      the principle underneath, by name when it has one
Shape · Pool · Link
```

- **The idea line uses the concept's name** when there is one (curse of knowledge, pronoia), which links the card to its concept page.
- **Each card is also tagged** as an observation, a principle, or both. Psychology mostly supplies principles; companies and the day-walk mostly supply observations; marketing supplies both.
- **Cards are stored raw.** Nothing on a card says who it's for, so changing the audience later breaks nothing.
- **No video-shaped card:** a line like "the belief it breaks" would put the system's reframe on every card. That help lives in the bridge step, on request.

## What you get

### The daily email

About 30 finds a day, balanced across shapes and pools, with roughly a quarter as wildcards outside the taste profile. It never shows the same find twice.

- **Top 5 to 7, in depth.** The system picks what it judges the most unique finds, the same idea as "if you read nothing else," and researches each properly.
- **The rest as short cards** beneath them.

### What you do

| When | What | Required? |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Daily | Reply "deeper 14" or "bridge 14" when something grabs you | No |
| Sunday | Answer one question in plain words: what did you use or like this week? | No |
| Anytime | `/creator-intelligence` to search the library | No |

- **Deeper** sends a new email with that card researched in depth.
- **Bridge** sends a new email with the three bridge options.
- **Plain language works,** so "go deeper on the Uber one" is fine.
- **Nothing breaks if you never reply.** The system keeps learning from what it can see on its own.

**Every email ends with a short reminder** of how to reply. Replies are comments on that day's GitHub issue, so the footer links to it. The daily footer:

```text
To reply, comment on today's issue: <issue link>
"deeper 14" researches card 14 in depth.
"bridge 14" shows ways to connect card 14 to your audience.
```

The Sunday footer links to that week's issue the same way:

```text
To reply, comment on this week's issue: <issue link>
Say what you used or liked this week, in your own words.
```

**Subjects:**

| Email | Subject |
| --- | --- |
| Daily | `Creator Intelligence · <Day Mon D> · <label of the top featured card>` |
| Deeper result | `Creator Intelligence · Deeper: <card title>` |
| Bridge result | `Creator Intelligence · Bridge: <card title>` |
| Weekly | `Creator Intelligence · Week of <Mon D>` (the Monday that starts the week reviewed) |
| Failure | `Creator Intelligence FAILED · <date>` |

The daily email's hidden preheader (the inbox preview text) is the labels of the featured cards joined by " · ".

### Pull mode

Search the whole library when you sit down to make something: `/creator-intelligence discipline`, `/creator-intelligence shocking-number finds about pricing`, `/creator-intelligence anything on pivots`. It searches every card ever saved and researches nothing new.

- **Exact searches** (shape, pool, company, concept) return precise matches.
- **Theme searches** ("discipline") need the model to judge relevance, since cards aren't filed by theme. It returns generously and says the count, such as "showing 15 of 40," so you can see past its ranking.

### Bridges, on request only

A bridge connects a find to a problem the audience has. It's opt-in so the system's take never anchors yours. Ask with "bridge 12" or through pull mode, and you get options deliberately of different kinds:

- **Flipped belief:** what the viewer has wrong
- **New frame:** a different way to see something
- **Lever:** a specific thing to do (this is where the actionable tip lives)

Each names the audience problem it aims at, plus the landing line. For the Uber chime:

```text
→ Can't stay consistent · Flipped belief
  You don't lack willpower, your environment has too much friction.
→ Wants to move faster · New frame
  Every step between you and the work is a decision you have to win again.
→ Can't stay consistent · Lever
  Remove the first step: sleep in your running clothes.
```

- **What a bridge is for:** sometimes good enough to use; otherwise it shows the obvious take so you know what to go past.
- **Audience problems are a lens, not a filing system.** `brain/audience-problems.md` is empty for now; until it's filled, each bridge picks the problem itself.
- **One off-list option** can aim at a problem not on the list.
- **A find can have no bridge.** It still belongs in the library.

## How it learns

The system improves at finding from everything you do, and diversity comes from its inputs so taste can steer it without collapsing it.

### Signals, without the upkeep

| Signal | Effort from you | What it teaches |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Asked to deepen | None, you wanted it | Worth more of your time |
| Asked to bridge | None | Interesting, but the use wasn't obvious |
| Searched for with `/creator-intelligence` | None | What you need when you sit down to make something |
| Its featured picks vs your actions | None | Deepening a card it didn't feature is a miss; a featured card you used is a hit |
| Self-review | None | Each find scored against the case-study pattern (familiar hook, real surprise, a principle that travels) and checked for whether it's already been told everywhere |
| Which bridge you picked, or that you wrote your own | None | How you like to land an idea |
| Used and liked, from the Sunday question | One optional answer a week | What actually became content. The strongest signal |

Silence is neutral: skipping the Sunday reply penalises nothing. "Used" is tracked at two levels: which **concepts** are big topics, and which **shapes** of find land.

### The brain: plain files you can read and correct

| File | Holds |
| --- | --- |
| Seen | Every find already shown, so nothing repeats |
| Taste profile | What interests you and why, in plain English |
| Scoreboard | How each strategy, shape, pool and source is performing |
| Strategies | The active search strategies, including contestants on trial |
| Shapes | The shape library, including shapes on trial |
| Leads | The queue of names to dig into |
| Sources | Seeds, contestants and discovered sources |
| Audience problems | Optional lens for bridges, empty for now |
| Signals | Dated log of every deepen, bridge, search and use |

### The weekly run

Sundays it does five things and sends one email.

1. **Asks one question:** what did you use or like this week? Answer in plain words ("the Uber one, the lace-making one") and it matches them to cards. To jog memory it lists only the week's featured finds and anything deepened or bridged, not all 200 cards. Skipping it is fine. The answer is processed by the reply run and logged as signals for next week.
2. **Rewrites the taste profile** from the week's signals.
3. **Scores the strategies,** retires weak ones once they have a month of data, and breeds variations of strong ones.
4. **Trials new shapes** when unshaped finds you engaged with start to cluster. Each is added automatically as a trial, no approval needed.
5. **Refills the leads** and promotes sources that keep producing good finds.

The same email says in a few lines what changed: strategies cut, shapes on trial, sources added, and the week's spread across pools and shapes.

### Guardrails

- **Wildcards:** about a quarter of every run ignores the taste profile, so it can't learn itself into a corner.
- **Taste isn't the only input.** Breadth comes from the spread of pools, shapes, strategies and sources.
- **No strategy or contestant source is cut until it has a month of data.**
- **Watch the used rate for three weeks.** Deepening plenty and using almost none means the finder works and the constraint is elsewhere.
- **Invented explanations** are the real risk for the hidden-reason shape. The source link is the check.
- **Shape balance** keeps videos from converging on one format.

## The line between the system and you

| Layer | Uber example | Who |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The find | The chime | System |
| The principle | Removing friction reinforces behaviour | System |
| Choosing what matters | Deepening or using this card | You |
| The bridge, including the actionable tip | "You don't lack willpower" / "sleep in your running clothes" | You, with options on request |

## Build

Same chassis as World Intelligence: GitHub holds everything, GitHub Actions runs the skills, Claude Code does the work.

**The engine and the output live apart.** Everything that runs (skills, schedules, the brain files) lives in the private repo `solopreneuur/creator-intelligence`. Everything you read and search (cards and concept pages) is written into the private repo `solopreneuur/second-brain`, under `creator-intelligence/`, so all material is indexed in one place and other skills there can use it later.

### Skills

| Skill | Lives in | Runs | Does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `ci-daily` | creator-intelligence | Every morning, scheduled | Picks leads, runs the competing strategies, writes about 30 cards to second-brain, updates concept pages and the seen list, writes the issue body |
| `ci-reply` | creator-intelligence | When you comment on a Creator Intelligence issue | Reads your reply, logs signals, posts "deeper" or "bridge" results as an issue comment, and emails the same result |
| `ci-weekly` | creator-intelligence | Sundays, scheduled | Asks what you used, rewrites the taste profile, scores and breeds strategies, trials shapes, refills leads, promotes sources |
| `creator-intelligence` | second-brain | When you ask | Pull mode: searches the library and bridges on request, and logs the search as a signal |

### Files

In `creator-intelligence`, the engine:

```text
brain/seen.md                 every find already shown
brain/taste-profile.md        plain-English model of your taste
brain/scoreboard.md           strategy, shape, pool and source performance
brain/strategies.md           active strategies and contestants
brain/shapes.md               the shape library
brain/leads.md                the queue of names to dig into
brain/sources.md              seeds, contestants, discovered sources
brain/audience-problems.md    optional bridge lens, empty for now
brain/signals.md              dated log of every deepen, bridge, search and use
.claude/skills/               ci-daily, ci-reply, ci-weekly
.github/workflows/            daily, reply, weekly
```

In `second-brain`, the output:

```text
creator-intelligence/cards/YYYY/MM/<card-id>.md   one card per file, with tags
creator-intelligence/concepts/<concept>.md        each concept and every card that anchors it
creator-intelligence/searches.md                  log of pull-mode searches (read by ci-weekly)
.claude/skills/creator-intelligence/SKILL.md      pull mode
```

Each card file carries its tags in frontmatter: id, date, shape, pool, concept, kind (observation, principle or both), source link, source name, the strategy that found it, featured (true or false), wildcard (true or false), and status (shown, deepened, bridged, used).

### The daily email and replies

Delivery matches World Intelligence: the email is sent by the workflow, not by GitHub.

- **The issue.** Each morning's cards are posted as a GitHub issue in `creator-intelligence` with the label `ci-daily` (labeled, not assigned). The Sunday question is posted the same way with the label `ci-weekly`. The issue is where replies go: commenting on it triggers the reply workflow.
- **The email.** Right after the issue is created, the workflow sends the same content as a styled email through Gmail with `.claude/scripts/send_email.py`. It reads `GMAIL_USER`, `GMAIL_APP_PASSWORD` and `EMAIL_TO` from the environment, turns the markdown into HTML with inline CSS so it reads cleanly on a phone, and adds the hidden preheader. The footer links to the issue.
- **Reply results.** The reply run posts its result as a comment on the issue and emails the same result with the matching subject.
- **Failures** send a failure email, not an issue.

### Carried over from World Intelligence

- **An outside clock.** cron-job.org starts the daily and weekly runs through `workflow_dispatch`, with GitHub's own schedule as a late backup.
- **An "already done today" check,** so a second trigger exits in seconds.
- **Working files outside `.claude/`** (use `_run/`), the workspace marked trusted in `~/.claude.json`, and allowed tools passed on the command line with `--allowedTools`. Do not use `--permission-prompts`.
- **Empty folders kept with `.gitkeep`,** and the issue labels created up front.
- **The reply workflow accepts only the repo owner's comments,** never acts on comments the bot itself posts, and treats the reply text as data, never as commands. The reply reaches Claude through a file, never pasted into a shell line.
- **Default model,** Opus at medium effort; no `--model` flag needed.
- **Styled email through Gmail** with the same `send_email.py` approach, including the hidden preheader, and with all whitespace (non-breaking spaces included) stripped from the app password before logging in.
- **Failure emails** say what failed, the exit code, the run link and the last 30 lines of the log.

### Notes for the build

- **Podcasts depend on transcripts.** Check that each can be fetched reliably from GitHub's machines before relying on it; fall back to show notes and web search when not.

## Decided against

- **Rating every card** (keep or skip). Too much upkeep; learning comes from natural actions instead.
- **Watching YouTube for what performs.** It turns a finder into a market-study tool.
- **Filing finds by audience problem.** The audience may shift.
- **A judge that cuts anything familiar.** Familiar hooks are the point.
- **A scoring formula** blending signals into one number.
- **Heavy fact-checking,** except for quotes.
- **A video-shaped card** with a "belief it breaks" line on every find.
- **A manual trial phase** before automating.
- **Cheap, surface-level digging** to save cost.
- **Steering toward thin areas.** Balance and wildcards already cover breadth.
