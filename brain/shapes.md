# Shapes

A shape is a way of looking that works across all three pools. The daily run
searches by shape, not by topic. Each card carries one shape slug in its
frontmatter (`shape:`), or `unshaped`.

Balance rule: no shape (on trial ones included, and `unshaped`) gets more than a
quarter of a day's cards.

## Active

### 1-hidden-reason · Familiar thing, hidden reason

- **Search angle:** "Why does [everyday product, moment or behaviour] do [this odd, specific thing]?"
- **Example:** The Uber chime. The sound at the end of a ride is the next ride being matched, which removes the step where a driver decides whether to keep going.

### 2-shocking-number · Simple experiment, shocking number

- **Search angle:** "Which experiment had a one-line setup and a single number that surprised everyone?"
- **Example:** The tapping study. Tappers predicted listeners would name 50% of the songs they tapped out; listeners named 2.5%.

### 3-wrong-decision-worked · A decision that looked wrong and worked

- **Search angle:** "What decision did the board, press or experts call a mistake, and what happened next?"
- **Example:** Avis's 1962 "We're No. 2, we try harder" campaign. It admitted to being second-best and turned a loss-making company profitable within a year.

### 4-tiny-change · Tiny change, outsized result

- **Search angle:** "What one-word, one-button or one-step change produced a measured result far bigger than the change?"
- **Example:** The "$300 million button." Replacing a checkout's "Register" button with "Continue", and an optional sign-up, lifted one retailer's purchases by 45%.

### 5-unknown-origin · Famous outcome, unknown origin

- **Search angle:** "What did [famous product, brand or habit] start out as, before anyone knew it?"
- **Example:** Bubble Wrap was invented in 1957 as textured wallpaper. It sold only after IBM used it to protect computers in shipping.

### 6-day-walk · Day-walk

- **Search angle:** Walk through an ordinary day (alarm, shower, commute, lunch, shopping, bed) and ask of each object and moment, "Why is it like this?"
- **Example:** Many lift "door close" buttons do nothing for passengers. They stay so the waiting feels like a choice.

### 7-far-field · Far-field

- **Search angle:** "What principle from an unrelated field (medicine, sport, military, aviation, biology, craft) carries over to business, marketing or the mind?"
- **Example:** The surgeon who learned stitching from lace-making.

### 8-unexpected-claim · Credible person, unexpected claim

- **Search angle:** "Which credible or famous person said something you would not expect from them, on a clip?"
- **Rule:** The quote must come from a real clip, video or podcast, with the timestamp. No clip, no card. The card carries the credential or the fame, whichever the source has, in a form ready to say out loud.
- **Example:** The Olympian crediting neuroplasticity.

### 9-unfamiliar-concept · Unfamiliar concept as the hook

- **Search angle:** "Which named idea most people have never heard of explains something they see all the time?"
- **Example:** Pronoia, the suspicion that people are secretly working to help you. Identity priming is another.

## Unshaped

Finds that fit no shape still get in, tagged `unshaped`. The weekly run watches
for clusters of unshaped finds you engage with. Those clusters are how new shapes
are found.
